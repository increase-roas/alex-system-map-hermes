# Alex System Map Strategy

## Purpose

Alex's agent setup is spread across files, apps, CLIs and machines. Finding contradictions and getting a repair into every affected place takes too many manual steps.

## Positioning

One dashboard follows each system's real source of truth and native repair tools. AI proposes and tests an exact repair, Alex approves once, and every affected local and remote target gets its own verification.

## Users

**Primary:** Alex, who wants to see how his whole agent setup connects and approve a tested fix without writing the replacement or coordinating machines himself.

## Boundaries

- Keep Alex's existing spending, real-person contact and client-production approval rules.
- Never claim success for a target that was not verified.
- Follow the system's canonical source and native owner rather than maintaining competing copies or routes.

## Key metrics

- **Approval clicks per repair**: the owner approval actions needed for an exact repair bundle, recorded in repair receipts.
- **Affected targets verified**: targets with successful source, copy and native readbacks compared with the approved plan, recorded per target in repair receipts.
- **Manual follow-ups left**: incomplete actions requiring Alex after the approved repair, recorded with their actual reason in repair receipts.

## Tracks

### Complete setup discovery

Make the real local and remote sources and their connections inspectable.

_Why it serves the approach:_ Alex can see the actual target and coverage before approving a repair.

### Native local and remote repairs

Apply one approved repair through the tools that own each target.

_Why it serves the approach:_ One approval can reach the source and its affected copies without asking Alex to carry out tool steps.

### Recovery and independent verification

Prove what changed, expose incomplete effects, and retain conditional recovery.

_Why it serves the approach:_ A successful-looking screen does not replace a readback from every affected system.
